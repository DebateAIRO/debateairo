# 3. Deterministic preflight architecture

## 3.1 Boundary

“Preflight” here means everything that can run once after a question arrives and before any runtime LLM judgement. It must not pretend to have classified the semantics already. It creates a typed envelope that makes later LLM calls small and prevents repeated work. This follows the plan’s recurring preference for frozen records, ledgers, counters, diffs and replayable arithmetic (Stages 1, 3–5, 9 and 11).

## 3.2 Order of operations

1. **Input and policy validation**
   - Normalize encoding and whitespace while preserving `original_text` byte-for-byte.
   - Validate requester/run identifiers, as-of date, jurisdiction/locale if supplied, and answer contract.
   - Load a versioned policy snapshot: human rules, lineage equivalence policy, subject-admissibility policy, search-amendment policy, abstention scale/price, confident-band enforcement, approved domain/type registries, and iteration budgets.
   - Fail closed on malformed required policy rather than spending tokens on a model to invent it.

2. **Identity, deduplication and cache lookup**
   - Hash original and normalized question plus policy/evidence-cutoff versions.
   - Look for an exact prior run, active answer, Stage 1 binding, query plan, opened-source archive, verified quote spans, instrument-validation receipt, and outcome row.
   - Cache hits are references, not silent truth: stale evidence and changed policy invalidate only affected downstream state.

3. **Capability and cost inventory**
   - Enumerate available retrievers, databases, file/code runners, network/access state, quotas, second-lineage availability, critic lineage, persistence backends and human decision owners.
   - Attach deterministic marginal costs (`tokens=0` for machine operations), hard resource limits and the Stage 6 iteration cap.
   - This directly seeds Stage 1 Q6 and prevents the LLM from guessing what can run.

4. **Literal question features—non-semantic only**
   - Extract dates, numbers, units, quoted strings, URLs/identifiers, explicit alternatives, named entities and requester-provided scope fields.
   - Do not call these a subject binding, question type, domain, value choice or presupposition; those are semantic judgements reserved for the LLM/human path.

5. **Existing-state integrity checks**
   - Verify hashes, timestamps, signatures, schema versions, locator syntax and referential integrity for reused artifacts.
   - Recompute persisted arithmetic and compare byte-identical runnable outputs where available.
   - Mark each artifact `valid`, `stale`, `policy_changed`, `missing`, or `corrupt`.

6. **Provisional activation plan**
   - Activate only unconditional structural prerequisites before semantic routing: Stage 1 intake; then routing judgement.
   - Compile conditional rules into an executable decision graph, but leave predicates depending on type, source count, measurement, split, agreement, recommendation or resolver as `UNKNOWN`, not `true`.
   - Once semantic fields arrive, the same engine activates rows without another planning prompt. This is the necessary correction to the conflicting “always-run” definitions in Part 1 and Part 9.

7. **Prompt-material compiler**
   - Build the first LLM request from only: original question, requester-provided fields, literal features, valid reusable state, policy enums, capability summary, and exact output schema.
   - Exclude the full plan prose, already-resolved ledgers, duplicate sources and auditor-only data.
   - Reserve stable IDs for every future claim, source, query, unknown, measurement, objection and decision so later calls exchange references rather than prose.

8. **Audit start and budget guard**
   - Persist the immutable preflight envelope and event `LLM_CALL_PENDING`.
   - Initialize token, retrieval, measurement, critic and retry counters. Enforce hard call/retry ceilings before dispatch.

After the first semantic call, this envelope grows into a `BatteryState` rather than being re-created: `intent`, `binding`, `assumptions`, `answer_rule`, `prior`, `settlement_act`, `question_type`, `domain`, `vantages`, `split_decision`, and the ledgers named throughout the plan. # 4. Top 10 token-cost reductions

The ranking is expected savings, not measured savings. Part 9 says retrieval dominates observed volume, failed retrieval consumes roughly 15–20% of retrieval spend, and every activation figure is a guess.

1. **Compile one executable activation graph and skip non-triggered questions.** This removes entire stages/calls for lookups, values questions, depth-zero questions and non-measurements, and resolves the exact 13-versus-43 contradiction (Part 1 “defect”; Part 9 activation model).
2. **Fetch, parse, archive and deduplicate sources once; cache verified spans by source/version hash.** Retrieval dominated one designer’s volume and repeated/failed retrieval is explicitly costly (Part 9 observations 1–2; Stage 4 Q15–19).
3. **Replace all search execution/accounting with a deterministic query runner and ledgers.** Running frozen queries, logging zero results and deriving absence are machine facts, not judgements (Stage 4 Q15 and Q17; Part 3 HARVEST).
4. **Make recombination, leverage, operator variants and fragility pure arithmetic.** Stage 9 says the alternative comparison costs nothing and sensitivity must be computed, while the engine’s hidden switch produced a 9.96-fold gap (Stage 9 Q45–49; Part 1 scoring-rule audit).
5. **Use a persistent typed state with stable IDs; never ask later stages to re-derive intent, scope, prior, unknowns, source interests or objections.** The plan already requires binding records and ledgers referenced downstream (Stage 1 Q1–6; Stage 3 Q12–14; Stage 8 Q44).
6. **Batch each irreducible semantic family into one structured call.** Produce assumptions/routing together, all queries together, all child falsifiers together, and all per-study rubric fields together, while keeping research and criticism in separate contexts (Stages 1–3, 6–8; Stage 8 law).
7. **Execute and validate measurements entirely outside the LLM.** Commands, raw output, replay, positive/negative fixtures and attempt history are deterministic and were the missing substance in the earlier mission (Stage 5 Q20–24; Parts 1 and 8).
8. **Compile serving from claim/provenance/objection/unknown ledgers and run machine gates.** Provenance proportions, visible strongest objection, movement arithmetic, typed abstention and over-abstention counters should not consume generation/review calls (Stage 10 Q51, Q53–56).
9. **Reduce cross-lineage review to unresolved semantic claims; machine-reopen sources and rerun sums first.** The critic should receive claim/span pairs, discrepancies and open objections—not the researcher’s full narrative—and independence is a receipt check (Stage 8 Q39–44; Part 9 notes retrieval/tool payloads dominate understated review totals).
10. **Persist outcomes and run Stage 11 as scheduled batch machine work.** Writes, proper scores, calibration statistics and liveness counters cost no runtime answer tokens and prevent every future confidence from being re-invented (Stage 11 Q59–62; Part 10’s missing ground-truth substrate).

# 5. Open questions only V can decide

These are policy/value questions, not implementation guesses. The first three are explicitly assigned to a human in Part 6.

1. **Frozen-query amendment policy:** must the original set remain absolute, or may a versioned, timestamped exploratory amendment run midstream, and may amendment results support confirmatory claims? (Part 6, decision 1; Human Rule 1.)
2. **Subject-admissibility policy:** should wholly/partly off-subject evidence be binary inadmissible or graded, including whether to adopt the plan’s suggested split—wholly off-subject inadmissible, partly relevant downgraded with reason? (Part 6, decision 2; Stage 7 Q32.)
3. **Abstention semantics and price:** define which endpoint of the 0–1 scale means what, choose the price, and identify whether it varies by question class/product risk. The battery has no authority to invent it. (Part 6, decision 3; Stage 1 Q6 and Stage 10 Q56.)
4. **Engine relationship:** does the battery replace the running engine, wrap it, or supersede only the older checklist while the engine is repaired separately? The introduction explicitly reserves this to the human seat.
5. **Lineage equivalence:** do different model generations from the same maker count as one lineage, and what about shared base models or providers? The glossary calls this unresolved and says the external-checking apparatus depends on it.
6. **Single-lineage enforcement:** confirm whether “no second lineage” means label-and-proceed, hold provisional, or block the confident band. The merged design chooses the middle, but Part 5 records three live positions and this is a product-risk policy.
7. **Approve, amend or reject Human Rules 6–9 and their enforcement:** all four are owner-added and unreviewed (Part 4). In particular, decide whether the stranger test is a hard serve block and what counts as substantive agreement between the two plain-topic sentences.
8. **Comparison/design value ownership:** who supplies criterion weights and accepts Pareto/conditional output for each product context? An LLM may identify criteria but may not manufacture value weights or a winner (Stage 2 type obligations; Stage 9 Q50).
9. **Battery liveness/removal threshold:** how many real runs, over which question classes, are sufficient before a never-firing check is demoted and then removed? Stage 11 Q62 supplies the law but not the threshold; battery size is explicitly unmeasured (Part 5).
10. **Expiry policy:** whether answers need automatic expiry in addition to a resolution/revision trigger, and what risk/volatility policy governs it. Parts 5 and 10 say the mechanism does not yet exist, so no designer can choose it by pretending to implement one.
11. **Validation/adoption bar:** what matched-cost experiment and outcome threshold must the battery pass before it can be called better than the older set or used in production? No battery has been compared against anything (Parts 5, 8 and 10).

# 6. Implications for the two final documents

## Human-readable report

The report must optimize for orientation and decision, not instrumentation. Its first layer should say, in ordinary language: what the battery is; that it is an unrun candidate; which judgements still require an LLM; which checks become free, deterministic machine work; the likely cost reductions without presenting the unmeasured ranking as measured fact; the strongest residual risks (semantic relevance, coverage, independent critique, expiry, and policy conflicts); and the exact decisions V must make. It should explain `MACHINE`, `LLM`, and `HYBRID` with a few representative examples, not reproduce implementation schemas. It must satisfy Human Rule 9: answer, certainty and revision trigger visible in the first paragraph, with no bare internal score. It also needs the caveat from Part 9 that “always-run” and activation counts do not reconcile.

## AI-catering companion

The companion must be executable in spirit: normative verdict for every question/rule; trigger predicate; required input types; deterministic function; smallest LLM output schema; state transitions; failure/downgrade/serve-block semantics; cache key and invalidation rule; critic-context boundary; provenance requirements; and test fixtures showing every gate fires both ways. It should include the preflight/BatteryState schemas, activation decision graph, enums, stable identifiers, operator registry, lineage policy hook, and read-after-write verification for outcome records. It must mark unresolved mechanisms as unresolved—especially coverage and expiry—and must not claim a character-level citation gate already exists. Unlike the human report, it should carry auditor detail below the fold: hashes, timestamps, locators, version constants, ledgers, arithmetic and liveness counters.

The two documents must agree on policy and verdicts but should not duplicate presentation. The human report explains what is being delegated and why; the companion tells an answering agent and implementation exactly what data to consume, what judgement (if any) to return, what code enforces afterward, and when not to call an LLM at all.
