## Reading rules and decision summary

The words **MUST**, **SHOULD**, and **COULD** express product requirements. They are recommendations rather than claims about the current product. Each RQ ends with one confidence statement and the strongest counter-argument; that statement applies to every recommendation in that RQ. Factual claims have an inline source. Where an official, current source could not establish a fact, the text says **UNVERIFIED**. Prices are public list prices checked on 2026-08-17, not quotes, and exclude tax, negotiated discounts, retries, implementation, support, and foreign-exchange effects unless stated otherwise.

The current product has no real authentication, accepts any non-empty user string, keeps client-controlled session identity, has no authentication-event audit trail, and exposes routes whose user/debate scope is attacker-controllable. Those facts make an MFA-only patch unsafe: the launch gate is a genuine server-verified identity/session and server-derived authorization scope first. The database is greenfield for credentials, and private debates are the launch default. These are local upstream findings, not fresh repository inspection in this seat. [Current-state brief](../../2026-08-17-accounts-privacy-security/wave-1-current-state.md) [Mission intake](../00-intake-H0.md)

**Recommended product posture:** target NIST AAL2-equivalent protection for access to private debates and security settings; require TOTP or a user-verified passkey in addition to a password for those paths; use email for address verification, alerts, and issued recovery codes but not as an authentication factor; ship WhatsApp only as an optional recovery address, never a sufficient recovery proof by itself; issue offline recovery codes; offer passkeys at launch if the selected managed-auth product meets the requirements; and make support unable to bypass the same recovery rules. NIST SP 800-63-4 is a US federal guideline, not automatically a legal mandate for this product; it is used here as the security benchmark. [NIST SP 800-63-4](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63.html)

## A. MFA methods, assurance, and protocol requirements

### A1 — NIST SP 800-63B-4 mapping and common-practice differences

SP 800-63-4 and its companion volumes were finalized in July 2025 and supersede revision 3. [NIST publication record](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/63/4/final) Its relevant classifications are:

| Method | NIST rev. 4 treatment | Requirement for this product |
|---|---|---|
| Password alone | A single-factor authenticator usable at AAL1; two instances of a knowledge factor are still one factor. [NIST identity model](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63/model/) | MUST NOT unlock private debates or security/recovery settings by itself. |
| Email link or email OTP | Email **shall not** be used for out-of-band authentication. Address-confirmation codes and issued recovery codes are explicitly outside that prohibition because they are not authentication processes. [NIST authenticator requirements](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/authenticators/) | MUST be labeled “verify email” or “recovery,” never “email MFA” or “NIST AAL2.” It MUST NOT be the only proof that restores an AAL2-protected account. |
| TOTP app | A single-factor OTP authenticator can be used at AAL1 alone and, with a distinct knowledge factor such as a password, at AAL2. Manual OTP entry is replay-resistant only when a value is accepted once, but is not phishing-resistant. [NIST AAL table](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/aal/) [NIST authenticator requirements](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/authenticators/) | MUST be combined with a password for sign-in, or used as one surviving authenticator in a recovery proof set. |
| Encrypted messaging-app code | NIST recognizes an encrypted instant-messaging service within out-of-band authentication only when the device/channel satisfies its unique authentication and protected-channel conditions. An arbitrary cloud messaging account or linked session is not automatically conformant. OOB authentication is not phishing-resistant. [NIST authenticator requirements](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/authenticators/) | WhatsApp MUST be treated as a recovery address, not claimed as an AAL authenticator, until a documented conformance assessment proves every NIST OOB property. Discord, Signal, Telegram, and WeChat MUST NOT be described as MFA. |
| Passkey/WebAuthn with user verification | Syncable cryptographic authenticators can satisfy AAL2 and provide verifier-name binding, replay resistance, intent, and multi-factor operation when user verification is required. AAL2 services must offer a phishing-resistant option. [NIST syncable authenticators](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/syncable/) [NIST AAL2](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/aal/) | SHOULD be the preferred user method and MUST be the privileged-user method once available. Require user verification; do not silently accept user-presence-only assertions for protected operations. |
| Recovery codes | Saved recovery codes are recovery methods, not routine login factors. At AAL2, recovery requires two differently delivered recovery codes, one recovery code plus a bound single-factor authenticator, or repeated identity proofing where the account was originally identity-proofed. [NIST authenticator events](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/events/) | MUST be combined with a password or another bound authenticator when restoring an MFA-protected account; a code alone MUST NOT restore it. |
| Security questions | KBA/security questions are not an acceptable authenticator. [NIST authenticator requirements](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/authenticators/) | MUST NOT be collected or used, even as a recovery “bonus” signal. See C5. |

Common practice often calls an email code or any app-delivered code “2FA.” That marketing convention conflicts with rev. 4: email is excluded as OOB authentication, and a messaging service only qualifies if the concrete device/channel properties are met. A password plus TOTP can meet AAL2 but is still phishable; AAL2 therefore requires that a phishing-resistant option be offered. [NIST authenticators](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/authenticators/) [NIST AAL2](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/aal/)

**Recommendation — confidence: high.** Use AAL2 as an engineering benchmark, state any deviations in a launch security record, and never use NIST assurance labels in user or audit output unless an end-to-end conformance assessment supports them. **Strongest counter-argument:** a private consumer debate service is not a federal system, and mandatory AAL2-like controls impose onboarding and recovery friction disproportionate to many accounts; risk-tiering low-value/read-only activity could achieve a better adoption trade-off.

### A2 — Exact TOTP interoperability and security profile

RFC 6238 defines `TOTP = HOTP(K,T)`, with `T = floor((UnixTime - T0)/X)`, defaults `T0 = 0` and `X = 30 seconds`, requires time values beyond 32 bits, permits SHA-1/SHA-256/SHA-512, recommends a 30-second step, and warns that a larger acceptance window enlarges attack exposure. It recommends at most one time step for network delay. [RFC 6238](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6238) RFC 4226 requires at least six digits and a secret of at least 128 bits, recommending 160 bits. [RFC 4226](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc4226)

Requirements:

- MUST use the maximum-interoperability profile: HMAC-SHA-1, six decimal digits, 30-second period, `T0=0`, and a uniformly random 160-bit secret per TOTP credential. SHA-1 here is the interoperable HMAC algorithm specified by HOTP, not a password hash. The reason to prohibit selectable algorithm/digit/period variants is that the de-facto `otpauth` documentation says Google Authenticator implementations ignore `algorithm` and `period`, and some ignore `digits`. [Google Authenticator key URI format](https://github.com/google/google-authenticator/wiki/Key-Uri-Format)
- MUST accept only counters `{T-1, T, T+1}` at enrollment and sign-in. This ±1 window is an explicit engineering judgment to tolerate small client clock skew; RFC 6238 directly supports a one-step past delay but does not prescribe a future-step allowance. A code accepted for a TOTP credential MUST be atomically marked by counter and MUST never be accepted again; accepting a new code MUST NOT erase the failure count. [RFC 6238](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc6238) [NIST authenticator requirements](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/authenticators/)
- MUST NOT silently widen the window. Repeated failure caused by drift MUST instruct the user to enable automatic time, offer another enrolled factor or recovery, and record a non-secret failure reason for operations. Clock resynchronization beyond ±1 is **UNVERIFIED** as interoperable across target apps and therefore is excluded at launch.
- MUST create a de-facto `otpauth://totp/` URI and a manual entry alternative. The URI MUST contain an unpadded Base32 secret, the same URL-encoded issuer in the label prefix and `issuer` parameter, and explicit `algorithm=SHA1&digits=6&period=30`. The account label MUST be human-recognizable but minimize exposure (prefer the user's chosen display label or a masked verified email); the issuer MUST be a stable `dezbatere.ro` label. The `otpauth` URI is de-facto ecosystem practice, not an RFC. [Google Authenticator key URI format](https://github.com/google/google-authenticator/wiki/Key-Uri-Format)
- MUST show QR and manual secret only inside a TLS-protected, freshly authenticated enrollment ceremony; MUST prevent intermediary caching; MUST never place the URI/secret in logs, analytics, crash reports, support transcripts, screenshots generated by the service, or LLM context. The verifier needs the seed to recompute codes, so it MUST be encrypted at rest under separately access-controlled key material; a password-style one-way hash alone is insufficient by design. This storage control is an engineering requirement; the precise key-management service is outside this brief.
- MUST require a recent password or user-verified passkey before initial binding, then require one valid TOTP code before activation. Binding or replacing TOTP on an already protected account MUST require fresh AAL2-equivalent authentication or a completed recovery flow; seeing a logged-in session alone is insufficient. Regeneration MUST create a new seed, revoke the old seed and its pending challenges, rotate recovery codes, revoke other sessions, and notify every previously verified address.
- MUST throttle TOTP by account and by coarse network/device abuse signals, starting no looser than five failures in five minutes and applying exponential delay capped at 15 minutes; MUST use generic errors and MUST NOT permanently lock an account solely from remote attempts. These numerical thresholds are launch engineering judgments and MUST be tuned from false-positive and abuse telemetry without revealing them to claimants.
- MUST test RFC 6238 Appendix B vectors for SHA-1/256/512 at the algorithm boundary, while product credentials remain SHA-1/6/30; MUST test 2038+ counters, Base32 case/padding variants, URL encoding, replay races, ±1 boundary transitions, recovery after drift, secret redaction, and at least current Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator, Aegis, and Bitwarden releases. Current cross-app behavior for every OS/version is **UNVERIFIED** until this compatibility suite is run.

**Recommendation — confidence: high for the RFC profile, medium for the ±1 operational window and throttle numbers.** Freeze SHA-1/6/30/160-bit as the launch interoperability contract and reject replay atomically. **Strongest counter-argument:** SHA-256 or eight digits is cryptographically stronger, and accepting a future step enlarges the guessing window; a smaller supported-app set could use stronger parameters or current-plus-previous only.

### A3 — Minimum secure launch enrollment, step-up, and session policy

Requirements:

- MUST replace the placeholder identity/session mechanism before MFA is credited. Account identity, tenant/user scope, and authorization context MUST come from a verified server-side session, never from a request body, query parameter, route parameter, or browser storage. This is required by the documented current-state defects. [Current-state brief](../../2026-08-17-accounts-privacy-security/wave-1-current-state.md)
- MUST verify an email address at registration, create a password or passkey through the chosen auth foundation, and require enrollment of either TOTP or a user-verified passkey before a user can create, view, export, or publish private debate data. Since private debates are the initial launch surface, this is effectively mandatory MFA for launch users. Email verification is not the second factor.
- MUST issue offline recovery codes and require at least one verified recovery address before completing MFA activation. SHOULD prompt for a second independent authenticator (a second passkey/device or TOTP credential) but MUST NOT require a phone number.
- MUST require a user-verified passkey for staff, administrators, and human recovery reviewers; TOTP MAY exist as a controlled break-glass fallback whose use creates a high-severity alert. A password plus TOTP is insufficient as the only privileged path because manual OTP is phishable. [NIST phishing-resistance requirements](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/authenticators/)
- MUST require fresh AAL2-equivalent step-up no older than five minutes for: adding/removing/replacing a factor; changing password, email, messaging address, or recovery contact; viewing/regenerating recovery codes; revoking other sessions; exporting or crypto-shredding private data; deliberately publishing a private debate; assigning a privileged role; and approving a recovery. Publishing is included because the upstream product ruling makes it a deliberate, potentially indexed disclosure. [Mission intake](../00-intake-H0.md)
- MUST use a secure server-bound session cookie, rotate session identifiers after authentication and privilege changes, provide “sign out all devices,” expose device/session history, and revoke all other sessions after recovery or suspected compromise. Browser storage MUST NOT be the source of authenticated identity.
- For AAL2-equivalent sessions, SHOULD set an overall reauthentication timeout of at most 24 hours and inactivity timeout of at most one hour, matching NIST's recommendation. A low-risk, read-only remembered-device experience MAY extend to 30 days only if it cannot access private data or security actions without fresh MFA; 30 days is an engineering convenience tier and is not NIST AAL2. [NIST AAL2 reauthentication](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/aal/)
- MUST send out-of-band security notices after factor, password, contact, recovery, role, and global-session changes. Notices MUST show time, coarse location/device information, and a safe “this wasn't me” protective action, but no credential, OTP, seed, full IP address, or private debate content.

Safe launch exclusions: email and messaging codes are excluded from routine MFA because they are phishable recovery channels, not necessary once interoperable TOTP exists; SMS/voice is excluded because it adds phone-number dependence and a restricted PSTN threat surface without closing an accessibility gap; ID/biometric recovery is excluded because identity was not bound at enrollment; and general-user passkeys may be deferred only if TOTP plus offline recovery and user-verified passkeys for privileged staff are complete. Social login, remembered-device access to private data, security questions, and discretionary support bypass add no assurance needed for the minimum posture and MUST remain excluded. The safety claim depends on TOTP availability, app-independent recovery codes, real server identity, and human recovery all shipping together.

**Recommendation — confidence: high on the security controls, medium on mandatory MFA for every private-data user and the timeout values.** Gate all private debate access behind MFA at the private launch. **Strongest counter-argument:** mandatory setup and daily reauthentication will increase abandonment and support load; optional MFA plus step-up only at publication/security changes may be adequate until the threat and sensitivity profile is measured.

### A4 — Passkeys: launch value, portability, and nontechnical users

WebAuthn scopes a credential to an RP ID/origin and exposes whether it is backup-eligible and currently backed up; those properties let a relying party distinguish multi-device and single-device credentials. [WebAuthn Level 3](https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-3/) FIDO calls cloud-synced credentials “synced passkeys” and single-device credentials “device-bound passkeys”; the device's local PIN or biometric approves use, and biometric material remains on the device. [FIDO passkeys](https://fidoalliance.org/passkeys-2/) Apple documents iCloud Keychain synchronization to approved devices. [Apple Passwords/passkeys](https://support.apple.com/en-us/120758)

Requirements:

- SHOULD offer passkeys at launch if the chosen managed-auth product passes RP-ID/origin, user-verification, backup-state, account-recovery, export/portability, and audit tests. If that would delay the private launch, MUST require them for privileged users at launch and deliver general-user passkeys in the first post-launch security milestone.
- MUST accept both synced and device-bound credentials; MUST record backup eligibility/state for risk and user education but MUST NOT reject a legitimate synced passkey merely because it is synced. MUST allow at least two passkeys per account and user-assigned device names.
- MUST require WebAuthn user verification for private data and sensitive actions, validate the exact expected origins and RP ID, and never make attestation-vendor identity a default requirement. WebAuthn warns that overly broad origin/subdomain handling can expose credentials to untrusted code. [WebAuthn Level 3](https://www.w3.org/TR/webauthn-3/)
- MUST explain in plain language where a passkey may be stored, how it may sync, that anyone able to unlock a device may be able to use it, and how to remove a lost device. Google gives the same lost/unlocked-device warning for account passkeys. [Google Account passkeys](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/17300380)
- MUST NOT make a passkey the sole recovery path. A synced passkey may survive device loss through the platform account; a device-bound passkey will not. Users MUST retain recovery codes or another independently controlled method.
- MUST support cross-device/hybrid use where the platform permits it, password-manager autofill, and a fallback to TOTP/recovery without dark patterns. MUST NOT infer or store the user's local biometric.

For many nontechnical users a passkey is simpler day to day because it reuses the familiar device-unlock action and removes transcription. Portability and recovery are harder to explain because behavior depends on the credential manager and whether the passkey is synced. That usability conclusion is an engineering inference from the cited platform behavior, not a measured result for this audience; product-specific completion and recovery rates are **UNVERIFIED** until usability testing.

**Recommendation — confidence: high on security value, medium on general-user launch timing.** Treat passkeys as the preferred factor and TOTP as the universal interoperable fallback. **Strongest counter-argument:** credential-manager fragmentation, shared-device ambiguity, and user confusion about syncing could create more launch recovery contacts than TOTP; delaying broad availability until usability testing may reduce risk.

### A5 — Phishing resistance and four-threat ranking

Legend: `strong` means the protocol directly resists the threat; `conditional` means surrounding account/device controls dominate; `weak` means the normal method is readily exposed. Manual OTP/OOB methods are not phishing-resistant under NIST. Properly configured WebAuthn passkeys are RP-bound and replay-resistant. [NIST authenticators](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/authenticators/) [NIST syncable authenticators](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/syncable/)

| Method | Static phishing | Real-time AiTM/proxy kit | SIM-swap/number takeover | OTP-bot social engineering | Stolen unlocked device | Overall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| User-verified passkey | Strong: RP binding prevents use at another domain. | Strong against credential relay when origin/RP validation is correct. | Strong: the credential is not phone-number based, although a weak parallel recovery path can still bypass it. | Strong: there is no transcribable code for the bot to solicit. | Conditional: device unlock may authorize use; remote revocation and multiple credentials matter. | 1 — best. |
| Password + TOTP | Conditional: a captured code expires. | Weak: password and live code can be relayed within the valid step. | Strong for normal use because TOTP is not delivered over PSTN; conditional if phone-number recovery can bypass it. | Weak: a claimant can be induced to read the current code to an attacker. | Conditional: separate authenticator helps unless the seed/app and password/session are jointly exposed. | 2. |
| Password + WhatsApp code | Conditional against later reuse; the code is short-lived. | Weak: password and code can be relayed. | Weak/conditional: takeover depends on the messaging provider's registration, linked-session, PIN, and number-reassignment controls, which are **UNVERIFIED** as a product-wide assurance set. | Weak: the user can be induced to disclose the code. | Weak when the unlocked device or linked messaging session exposes the code. | 3, recovery-only here. |
| Password + email code/link | Conditional against later reuse. | Weak to relay and mailbox-session capture. | Conditional: email is not inherently number-based, but mailbox recovery may depend on a phone and that dependency is not visible to this product. | Weak: a user can be induced to disclose a code or activate a link. | Weak if the same device has the mailbox. | 4, recovery-only here. |
| Saved offline recovery code + password | Conditional: a single-use code can still be phished. | Weak to live relay, but exposure is limited to one use. | Strong when stored independently of the phone. | Weak if the user can be persuaded to disclose it; support must never request it. | Strong if stored off-device; weak if stored on the stolen device. | Emergency method, not routine sign-in. |
| Security questions | Weak: stable answers can be solicited. | Weak. | Weak/irrelevant: they add no device possession. | Weak: a bot or human can solicit or guess the answer. | Weak/irrelevant. | Prohibited. |

The table's device/channel judgments are threat-model inferences. Their population frequencies are **UNVERIFIED** for this product. Among V's proposed methods, email and messaging codes are the weakest links because they are phishable, relayable, and often recoverable through the same phone/device. Their compensating controls MUST be: recovery-only labeling; short-lived, purpose-bound, single-use codes; transaction and attempt throttles; no reset links in chat; an independent bound password/authenticator or differently delivered code; dependency detection; cooling-off; notices to old channels; contest/cancel; and sensitive-action holds. These controls reduce risk but do not make email or messaging phishing-resistant MFA.

**Recommendation — confidence: high.** Prefer passkeys, then password+TOTP; keep recovery codes offline; do not present WhatsApp or email as phishing-resistant MFA. **Strongest counter-argument:** a passkey's security advantage can be undermined by the same weak recovery path, while familiar TOTP or messaging codes may achieve higher real-world adoption and therefore protect more accounts.

### B2 — Country, regulatory, and censorship constraints

Observed examples prove that no messaging app can be a global mandatory path:

- China blocks many international messaging platforms, including WhatsApp, Signal, Telegram, and Discord's peers; Freedom House also reports removal of WhatsApp, Telegram, and Signal from China's Apple App Store in 2024. [Freedom House China report](https://freedomhouse.org/country/china/freedom-net/2024) Weixin and WeChat have distinct mainland/international positioning. [Tencent product description](https://www.tencent.com/products/weixin-wechat/)
- India formally blocked WeChat among 59 apps in 2020. [Government of India release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1635206)
- OONI measurements indicate Discord blocking began in Russia and Türkiye on 8 October 2024 and remained ongoing at the report date. [OONI Russia/Discord finding](https://explorer.ooni.org/findings/162112071200)
- Russia attempted to fully block WhatsApp in February 2026. [Associated Press](https://apnews.com/article/fe9389db480460f0cd74fd67a058d070)
- Twilio lists business-platform prohibitions/restrictions including Cuba, Iran, North Korea, Syria, and Crimea/Donetsk/Luhansk examples. [Twilio restricted-country error](https://www.twilio.com/docs/api/errors/63058)

The examples are not a complete or permanent availability list. Current access for every country, carrier, operating system, age group, and sanctions regime is **UNVERIFIED** and inherently volatile.

Requirements:

- MUST NOT require any messaging app, phone number, app store, or single foreign platform for registration, MFA, recovery, or security alerts.
- MUST maintain a dated country/channel capability register from provider production responses and authoritative legal/censorship sources; MUST fail over to offline recovery codes, TOTP/passkey, email recovery-code delivery, or human review without revealing whether an account exists.
- MUST make channel selection country-aware but MUST NOT infer legal eligibility from IP geolocation alone. Users traveling, using VPNs, or holding foreign numbers MUST be able to choose another channel.
- MUST obtain sanctions/export-control and local communications/privacy review before enabling business messaging in a jurisdiction that the provider flags. Exact applicability to the future legal entity and launch countries is **UNVERIFIED — counsel decision required**.

**Recommendation — confidence: high.** Make app-independent recovery mandatory and gate each messaging integration by a live country capability policy. **Strongest counter-argument:** maintaining legal and delivery policy by country is expensive at private-launch scale; a simpler universal TOTP/email/recovery-code launch could defer all messaging until priority markets are known.

### B3 — Compromise, circular trust, and phone-number dependence

An app-delivered code proves current control of the addressed messaging account/session, not necessarily control of one physical device or the identity of the original registrant. That conclusion is a threat-model inference. NIST only treats encrypted instant messaging as OOB authentication when the concrete device/channel authentication conditions are met, and says manual OOB secrets are not phishing-resistant. [NIST authenticator requirements](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/authenticators/)

Requirements:

- MUST model at least: stolen unlocked phone; malware/notification preview; linked desktop/web session; messaging-account password compromise; social recovery by the messaging provider; phone-number recycling, porting, or SIM swap where the app is phone-bootstrapped; cloud backup restore; OAuth/bot token compromise; malicious/removed bot; provider moderation or account ban; and phishing of the issued code.
- MUST NOT count two destinations as independent recovery methods when they depend on the same phone number, email inbox, device, platform account, or password-manager/cloud account. The user-visible security page SHOULD disclose detected dependency without revealing it to an unauthenticated claimant.
- WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal, or WeChat possession MUST NOT override loss of the password plus all enrolled authenticators. Messaging plus password may count as one issued recovery code plus a bound password under the NIST AAL2 pattern, but this product SHOULD still apply device/risk checks and a sensitive-action hold because generic messaging conformance is unproven. [NIST recovery methods](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/events/)
- MUST impose a cooling-off period on a newly added/changed phone or messaging address; notices MUST go to all old verified channels; the old owner MUST be able to cancel the pending change without signing in; support and bots MUST NOT shorten the period.
- MUST never ask a user to paste an OTP, recovery code, password, seed, or provider login into support chat. A support message that contains one MUST be automatically redacted from routine views and treated as compromised.
- PSTN/SMS fallback, if a provider introduces it, MUST be separately approved as a restricted authenticator with number-port/SIM/device-swap signals and a non-PSTN alternative. NIST explicitly restricts PSTN OOB and calls for those signals. [NIST authenticator requirements](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/authenticators/)

**Recommendation — confidence: high.** Treat messaging as a weak, potentially circular recovery address and require an independent proof set. **Strongest counter-argument:** requiring multiple independent proofs will strand users whose phone is their only computing device; for low-value pseudonymous accounts, a delayed password-plus-WhatsApp recovery may be a better harm trade-off.

## C. Recovery design, loss/compromise cases, risk, privacy, and access

### C1 — Recovery-code enrollment, second factors, and retention assumptions

NIST requires a saved recovery code to contain at least 64 random bits, be kept offline by the user, be throttled and stored as a one-way hash, be invalidated after use, and cause a replacement/notification. It requires an AAL2 account to recover with two codes delivered by different method, one recovery code plus a bound single-factor authenticator, or repeated identity proofing if identity proofing was performed originally. [NIST authenticator events](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/events/)

Requirements:

- MUST issue ten independent, human-enterable recovery codes, each containing at least 128 uniformly random bits before encoding. Ten and 128 bits are conservative product choices above NIST's minimum, not a NIST mandate.
- MUST display the full set only once through a freshly AAL2-authenticated page; offer accessible copy, plain-text download, print, and password-manager save instructions; explicitly warn against storing the only copy on the TOTP phone. MUST NOT email the set, add it to analytics, log it, show it to support, or permit later full reveal.
- MUST store only slow/one-way or keyed one-way verifiers plus per-code state, never recoverable plaintext. Each code MUST be single-use and rate-limited. Successful use MUST invalidate that code; viewing or generating a new set MUST invalidate every old unused code; completing recovery MUST rotate the whole set and notify all prior verified addresses.
- MUST require the user to acknowledge storage and enroll at least one verified recovery address or second authenticator before MFA activation. A checkbox does not prove real retention, so the product MUST continue to prompt periodic recovery-readiness checks and show the count of remaining codes only inside a fresh-MFA session.
- SHOULD let users register multiple passkeys and, where the auth foundation permits, more than one TOTP credential with distinct names. MUST warn if two “different” paths are detected on the same device/platform, but MUST NOT block shared-device users solely on that inference.
- MUST NOT assume recovery-code retention. A reliable published rate for consumer users retaining and later finding codes was **UNVERIFIED — no checkable product-relevant study was found**. Launch readiness MUST therefore be measured by: codes generated, save-method chosen, second authenticator enrolled, recovery drill completion, later recovery success, abandonment, accessibility escalation, and false takeover reports. The drill MUST use a disposable challenge, not consume or reveal an actual saved code to telemetry.
- SHOULD ask users every 12 months and after a factor/contact change to confirm that at least two independent recovery paths remain, without forcing code disclosure. Twelve months is an engineering cadence, not evidence of optimal retention.

**Recommendation — confidence: high on code properties, medium on issuing ten and the annual cadence.** Make offline codes plus a second independent path a completion gate rather than an optional afterthought. **Strongest counter-argument:** users often misplace codes, and forcing a recovery setup they do not understand can create false confidence and onboarding abandonment; one NIST-style code plus multiple passkeys may be simpler.

### C2 — Exhaustive loss-case policy, proof bar, time, and aftermath

For this matrix, a **strong proof** is a successful bound password, TOTP, passkey, or saved recovery code. An **issued proof** is a single-use recovery code sent to a pre-verified email/WhatsApp address. A familiar device, IP/ASN, location consistency, account age, and knowledge of account history are contextual risk signals, not authenticators and never sufficient alone. These categories are product definitions informed by NIST's recovery classes. [NIST authenticator events](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/events/)

The target times below are launch service requirements/engineering judgments, not published guarantees.

| Loss case | Required proof bar | Target path/time | Mandatory completion effects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lost TOTP phone; recovery codes available | Password or surviving passkey **plus** one saved recovery code. If the password is also lost, require the saved code plus a different issued code or recovery contact. | Self-service; target under 5 minutes. | Revoke the lost TOTP seed and all other sessions; bind a new factor; rotate all recovery codes; notify every old address; hold contact/factor removal and publication/export/deletion for 24 hours. |
| Lost TOTP phone and saved codes; password and verified address survive | Password **plus two independent issued/recovery-contact codes**, or password plus a surviving passkey/second TOTP. Email and WhatsApp sharing one compromised device/phone account do not count as independent. | Self-service if two strong/issued paths and low risk, otherwise human review; 24–72 hour hold. | Same revocation/rotation/notices; cancellation link to every old address; 72-hour sensitive-action hold if only issued channels were used. |
| Lost recovery email; normal MFA survives | Fresh password + TOTP, or a user-verified passkey. The new email must be separately confirmed. | Self-service; target under 10 minutes, but new address remains pending for 24 hours. | Notify old and new addresses; old address can cancel during the hold; do not reveal the full old address; rotate sessions after activation. |
| Lost WhatsApp/phone/messaging account; normal MFA survives | Fresh password + TOTP, or a user-verified passkey. Possession of a new phone number is not proof of ownership. | Self-service; target under 10 minutes, 24-hour activation hold. | Notify old email and messaging address, invalidate outstanding messages/codes, and prohibit the new channel from recovery until the hold ends. |
| Lost one passkey/device; another authenticator survives | Fresh surviving passkey, or password + TOTP. | Self-service; target under 5 minutes. | Remove only the named lost credential, revoke sessions on the lost device where identifiable, notify, and encourage registration of another passkey. |
| Stolen device but a different factor/channel survives | Use a different device and two proofs (e.g. password + recovery code/passkey); allow unauthenticated protective freeze with non-enumerating response. | Freeze immediately; recovery target under 30 minutes if proof succeeds. | Revoke all sessions, lost-device passkeys/TOTP/messaging factor, and outstanding recovery transactions; rotate password if exposed and recovery codes; 72-hour high-impact-action hold. |
| Lost password but MFA survives | This is binding a replacement password, not full account recovery under NIST. Require TOTP/passkey plus an issued recovery code to a pre-verified address. [NIST events](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/events/) | Self-service; target under 10 minutes. | Rotate session secrets, notify, and review/revoke unknown devices. |
| Lost everything: no password, factor, code, verified address, trusted session, or pre-enrolled recovery contact | **No instant restoration and no support override.** A fresh ID is not proof that the holder owned a pseudonymous account. Human review may look for pre-bound, non-secret enrollment evidence, but contextual account trivia/private debate knowledge is insufficient. If the account was identity-proofed at enrollment, repeated equivalent proofing may be used; otherwise the secure result may be permanent lockout. | Protective freeze immediately. Human acknowledgement within one business day; minimum 7-day contested hold; target a reasoned decision in 7–14 days, not guaranteed restoration. | Notify all historical verified addresses, preserve audit evidence, prevent publication/export/deletion during contest, and require new credentials plus global revocation if approved. If proof stays below threshold, deny access and offer a separate privacy-rights channel without disclosing account content. |
| User is deceased/incapacitated | No ad-hoc login recovery. Require a separately approved legal/estate process; product policy is **UNVERIFIED — counsel and V must decide whether such access exists**. | Human/legal only; no target until policy exists. | Never disclose private debates or credentials merely from kinship claims; preserve/deal with data under the deletion/publication rulings and applicable law. |

Global loss-flow requirements:

- MUST present the least invasive available proof path first and MUST NOT collect ID/biometrics for a case that can be solved with bound authenticators or recovery codes.
- MUST use single-use, purpose-bound, expiring recovery transactions; prevent parallel requests from racing; rate-limit by account, address, device, and coarse network; and keep outward responses account-enumeration resistant.
- MUST permit the legitimate user to cancel a pending weak recovery from an old verified channel or surviving session. Cancellation may freeze the account; it MUST NOT silently restore a potentially compromised old factor.
- MUST NOT crypto-shred private data, publish content, or permanently remove audit evidence during a contested recovery. The upstream ruling makes crypto-shredding irreversible for private deletions, so recovery and deletion must be separate state machines. [Mission intake](../00-intake-H0.md)
- MUST show users before MFA activation that “lost everything” can mean permanent loss unless they keep two independent paths. That fail-closed policy is a product decision, not an industry fact.

**Recommendation — confidence: high on proof independence and fail-closed support, medium on 24/72-hour and 7-day timers.** Provide fast recovery only from pre-bound evidence; do not invent identity after the account is lost. **Strongest counter-argument:** permanent lockout is severe for a private-data product, and an extended human/contextual review may recover legitimate users even though it creates a takeover path; V may prefer that availability risk.

### C3 — Exhaustive compromise-case policy and reversible/irreversible actions

The system MUST record and evaluate, at minimum: password-reset and recovery starts; password/TOTP/code failures and subsequent success; replay of a TOTP counter, issued code, recovery code, or revoked session; credential-stuffing velocity; new device/browser and coarse IP/ASN/geovelocity anomaly; concurrent distant sessions; passkey backup-state change where available; new factor/contact/session binding; factor/contact removal; global sign-out; provider risk/outage callbacks; notice delivery/bounce; user contest/freeze; and support/bot boundary denials. A single context signal MUST NOT prove compromise or ownership; it selects containment or a stricter path. These are defensive engineering requirements, and their product-specific predictive value is **UNVERIFIED until measured**. Private debate text and political/identity inference MUST NOT be detection inputs.

| Compromise case | Required immediate response | Recovery proof and state change | What the claimant may/cannot undo |
|---|---|---|---|
| Password only compromised | Rate-limit and challenge MFA; notify on suspicious successful password followed by failures; offer password reset from an authenticated session or recovery flow. | Existing user signs in with password + TOTP/passkey, changes password, and reviews sessions. | User can change password and revoke sessions. They cannot erase the security-event record or reset abuse counters. |
| Password + one TOTP/WhatsApp factor compromised | Treat as probable full takeover if both are a valid sign-in pair. Place a protective freeze, revoke sessions, suspend high-impact actions, and alert all old channels. | Require a different pre-bound proof set or human recovery; the compromised pair cannot approve its own replacement. | Old owner can cancel pending contact/factor/publication changes from a prior channel. Neither side can shorten the investigation hold. |
| Email inbox compromised | Email reset/issued code MUST NOT bypass MFA. Freeze pending email/factor changes initiated from that inbox if anomaly signals exist. | Require password + TOTP/passkey/recovery code independent of email; change email only after step-up and hold. | User may cancel pending email replacement through a surviving factor. Cannot remove notices/audit evidence. |
| WhatsApp/messaging account compromised | Invalidate outstanding app codes; mark channel unusable; do not disclose other factors or addresses in chat/message. | Require non-messaging strong proofs; rebind only after fresh MFA and cooling period. | User may remove channel. Cannot count the compromised channel in the current recovery. |
| Device lost or compromised | Offer a non-enumerating remote freeze; revoke sessions/tokens on that device and assume local passkeys, TOTP, mailbox, and messaging may be jointly exposed if the device was unlocked. | Require evidence not residing solely on that device, then bind replacements and rotate all recovery material. | User can cancel an erroneous freeze after strong authentication. Cannot revive revoked session tokens or old recovery codes. |
| Saved recovery code disclosed but unused | Invalidate entire set after fresh MFA and generate a new set; alert. | No account recovery needed if normal MFA survives. | User cannot reactivate the old set. |
| Active session cookie stolen | Revoke session family and all sessions on evidence of reuse; require full MFA rather than session-only reauthentication. | Review devices/actions, reset affected credentials if changes occurred. | User can restore access through normal auth; cannot reinstate the stolen session. |
| Auth/email/messaging provider breach or outage | Disable affected assurance contribution, stop retries that amplify impact, preserve evidence, and switch to independent methods. | Apply a provider-wide risk flag; do not punish users with permanent lockout before human review. | User cannot force continued use of a disabled provider; can choose a healthy enrolled method. |
| Support-agent or bot compromise | Remove the principal's access, freeze affected cases, review every read/action, and require independent human/security approval for remediation. | Bots never have recovery grant authority, limiting restoration to human policy. | Users can contest changes; compromised agents cannot erase logs, approve their own cases, or restore access. |

Notifications MUST go to all previously verified addresses, not only the newly changed one; include timestamp, action, coarse device/location, case ID, and a safe cancel/freeze action; and exclude secrets, full identifiers, private debate text, risk-score internals, and clickable login links where a typed domain/bookmark instruction is safer. Google exposes recent security activity and unknown-device handling, and Microsoft notifies original security information during its 30-day change hold; these are useful patterns, not requirements to copy exactly. [Google compromised-account guidance](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/6294825) [Microsoft security-info hold](https://support.microsoft.com/en-us/accounts-billing/manage/what-does-security-info-change-is-still-pending-mean)

System-enforced invariants that no user, bot, or ordinary support agent can undo: append-only auth/recovery audit; used/revoked code and token state; rate-limit history; minimum risk holds; human-review provenance; notifications already dispatched; and the rule that a compromised proof cannot approve itself. User-reversible protective actions: cancel a pending contact/factor change; contest recovery; freeze/unfreeze after strong proof; revoke devices; and withdraw a recovery request. Destructive private-data deletion remains irreversible only after its separate confirmed/cooling process completes.

**Recommendation — confidence: high.** Make compromise recovery revoke broadly, notify old channels, and preserve contested state; prefer temporary protective denial over irreversible change. **Strongest counter-argument:** global revocation and multi-day holds impose large collateral damage on users whose alert was a false positive or whose only other device is unavailable.

### C4 — Risk signals, fast/slow recovery, major-platform lessons, and build/buy

#### Evidence from established services

The defensible transferable pattern is: prefer surviving bound authenticators and familiar context; slow or block anomalous recovery; notify old channels; let the old owner contest; and do not let support waive the controls. Fintech ID/selfie recovery is not directly transferable because the fintech bound legal identity during onboarding, while this debate product is presently pseudonymous. That distinction is an inference from the cited flows and local product brief.

#### Risk decision requirements

- MUST build a deterministic first-party recovery policy using product-owned signals. Strong proofs: bound passkey, password, TOTP, saved recovery code, issued code to a pre-verified address, and pre-enrolled recovery contact. Context-only signals: signed session, familiar device cookie, device/OS continuity, IP/ASN reputation, coarse geovelocity, account age, usual login timing, recent password/factor/contact changes, repeated recovery attempts, breached-password evidence, and prior successful recovery. Context MUST NOT silently become an authenticator.
- MUST use three outcome bands: **fast** (required NIST-like proof set, no high-risk anomaly; immediate credential restoration plus 24-hour sensitive-action hold), **slow** (minimum proof met but context conflicts; 48–72-hour contestable hold and human review), and **insufficient** (proof below minimum; freeze/deny, or 7-day-plus review if pre-bound evidence might be found). The bands/times are engineering judgments and MUST be versioned and auditable.
- MUST never use nationality, ethnicity, political/religious content, debate position, protected/special-category inferences, precise location history, contact graph, or biometric demographic inference as a positive/negative recovery signal. MUST test error rates across device accessibility, country, language, and chosen auth method; provide human appeal without exposing threshold details.
- MUST keep risk scoring separate from authorization: the score selects a stricter path but cannot itself grant access. Every grant must enumerate the verified proofs and policy version.
- MUST build the orchestration/risk policy because it depends on first-party enrollment history and product semantics. MAY buy commodity auth/session and document/liveness checks. MUST NOT buy an opaque “account ownership score” that cannot provide reason codes, deletion, appeal, regional processing, audit, and independent validation.

**Recommendation — confidence: high on building first-party policy and not using fresh ID for pseudonymous ownership; medium on the three time bands and vendor shortlist.** Buy commodity ID/liveness only if a later, pre-bound identity recovery policy requires it. **Strongest counter-argument:** mature IDV vendors aggregate fraud signals and review expertise the team cannot reproduce; rejecting fresh ID may cause avoidable permanent loss and encourage duplicate accounts/abuse.

### C5 — Secret questions: evidence and final verdict

NIST rev. 4 prohibits prompting for KBA/security questions. [NIST authenticator requirements](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/authenticators/) OWASP states there is no acceptable use for security questions in secure software and that recovery must be no weaker than normal authentication. [OWASP security questions](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Choosing_and_Using_Security_Questions_Cheat_Sheet.html) Google's large deployment study found personal questions weaker than user passwords; 40% of English-speaking US users could not recall answers when needed, and 37% of users who admitted faking answers did so predictably. [Google research](https://research.google/pubs/secrets-lies-and-account-recovery-lessons-from-the-use-of-personal-knowledge-questions-at-google/)

Requirements:

- MUST NOT collect, store, prompt, import, or infer security questions/answers.
- MUST NOT use biographical or debate-history trivia as a partial recovery score. The Google paper's conclusion leaves some room for questions combined with other signals, which is the strongest published argument for partial use, but this product would add sensitive personal data and a phishable knowledge factor for marginal evidence.
- MUST delete any inadvertently collected question/answer from support notes and logs under the incident/deletion process, while retaining a non-secret audit fact that prohibited data was removed.
- MUST fill the usability gap with offline recovery codes, multiple passkeys/TOTP devices, pre-enrolled recovery contacts/addresses with cooling periods, accessible autofill/copy, familiar-device context used only to slow—not grant—and a human appeal that cannot override proof minimums.

**Recommendation — confidence: high.** Prohibit secret questions even as a weak signal. **Strongest counter-argument:** the Google study says questions can retain some value when combined with other signals, and in a lost-everything case any incremental evidence may improve legitimate recovery; the privacy and social-engineering cost still outweighs that value here.

### C6 — GDPR, US biometric laws, global launch, and prohibited processing

This is requirements research, not legal advice. The future legal entity, controller/processor roles, target countries, minors policy, vendor regions, and identity-binding purpose are unresolved, so exact legal applicability is **UNVERIFIED — qualified counsel must decide before processing**. The following are checkable legal constraints that create the launch gate:

- GDPR defines biometric data as personal data from specific technical processing of physical, physiological, or behavioral characteristics that allows/confirms unique identification. Article 9 treats biometric data used for unique identification as a special category and prohibits processing unless an exception applies. Article 35 requires a DPIA before processing likely to create high risk and specifies its minimum contents. A mere photo is not automatically biometric under that definition; face matching/liveness designed to identify is the relevant processing. [GDPR official text](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)
- The EDPB says a DPIA must precede likely high-risk processing and must cover description/purpose, necessity/proportionality, risks, and mitigations; unresolved high residual risk requires supervisory-authority consultation. [EDPB DPIA guidance](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/topics/accountability-and-compliance-tools/data-protection-impact-assessment_en)
- Illinois BIPA requires a public retention/destruction policy, written notice of collection/purpose/term, written release, restrictions on sale/disclosure, and protection at least as strong as other confidential information; destruction is required when the purpose is satisfied or within three years of the last interaction, whichever is earlier. [740 ILCS 14/15](https://www.ilga.gov/Documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/074000140K15.htm) It provides a private action and statutory damages of `$1,000` for negligent and `$5,000` for intentional/reckless violations or actual damages, plus fees/other relief; the 2024 amendment treats repeated same-method collection/disclosure from the same person as a single violation for those subsections. [740 ILCS 14/20](https://www.ilga.gov/Documents/legislation/ilcs/documents/074000140K20.htm)
- Texas requires notice and consent before commercial capture of biometric identifiers. [Texas Business & Commerce Code ch. 503](https://statutes.capitol.texas.gov/?artSec=503.001&chapter=BC.503&code=BC&tab=1) Washington separately regulates enrollment, disclosure, and retention of biometric identifiers. [Washington RCW 19.375](https://app.leg.wa.gov/RCW/default.aspx?cite=19.375&full=true) Colorado's biometric amendments took effect 1 July 2025 and require written policy, consent/disclosure, retention/deletion, and incident provisions. [Colorado HB24-1130](https://www.leg.colorado.gov/bills/hb24-1130) These examples demonstrate a changing state patchwork; a complete 50-state and non-US survey is **UNVERIFIED**.

Requirements before any document, selfie, liveness, face match, or biometric template is collected:

- MUST document purpose, necessity, proportionality, lawful bases (including a valid Article 9 condition where applicable), jurisdiction scope, controller/processor roles, data map, transfers, vendors/subprocessors, retention/deletion, security, accessibility, bias/error evaluation, human appeal, incident response, and whether service is genuinely available without biometric processing.
- MUST complete a DPIA and counsel review before production. If relying on consent, MUST prove it is freely given, specific, informed, unambiguous, granular, withdrawable without deceptive friction, and not bundled with unrelated service; whether consent is valid for a necessary-only recovery gate is **UNVERIFIED — counsel**.
- MUST provide jurisdiction-appropriate written notice before capture, capture affirmative/written release where required, publish retention/destruction policy, and retain proof of notice/consent separately from raw biometric material.
- MUST minimize to the recovery transaction; encrypt in transit/at rest; restrict staff with just-in-time access and complete view/export audit; prefer vendor-hosted capture so the application never receives raw images; and delete raw ID/selfie/liveness promptly after decision/appeal. Exact prompt deletion targets are **UNVERIFIED pending vendor capability and counsel**; contract terms MUST state them and provide deletion evidence.
- MUST provide an accessible non-biometric alternative and meaningful human appeal. A failed or unavailable biometric must not automatically permanently deny the account if an equivalent pre-bound proof path exists.
- MUST prohibit collection through ordinary email, Bot A/B chat, ticket attachments, or staff personal devices; prohibit putting images/templates/ID numbers in application logs, analytics, crash reports, debate datasets, search indexes, or LLM prompts; and segregate recovery evidence from debate content.

Prohibited practices:

- No default or speculative identity proofing “in case recovery is needed”; no fresh-ID claim that a document holder owns a previously pseudonymous account; no face search against public/private debate media; no biometric categorization, emotion/ethnicity/gender/political inference, contact-graph enrichment, or reuse for moderation/advertising.
- No sale, marketing, cross-customer fraud graph, vendor model training, human-review training, or product improvement using recovery documents/biometrics unless a separate, specific, legally approved basis and opt-in exists. The launch default is prohibition.
- No indefinite retention; no silent transfer to a new region/subprocessor; no raw export by ordinary support; no shared staff accounts; no automatic denial without reason category and appeal; and no deletion that prevents an active legal hold or appeal without documented reconciliation.
- No production biometric recovery until target US states and every launch country have a dated counsel matrix. “Global day one” does not permit assuming GDPR or BIPA is the only applicable regime.

**Recommendation — confidence: high on the privacy/security gates, medium on legal scope because entity/jurisdictions are unknown.** Do not launch biometric/ID recovery; preserve it as a later, counsel-gated option only if identity is bound at enrollment and non-biometric access remains. **Strongest counter-argument:** liveness/document verification may be the only scalable evidence for lost-everything cases and can reduce social-engineering against support; excluding it shifts harm to permanent lockout.

### C7 — Accessibility and exclusion-resistant recovery

WCAG 2.2 AA criterion 3.3.8 requires an authentication path that does not demand an unaided cognitive-function test; password-manager assistance, copy/paste, or a non-cognitive alternative can satisfy it. The criterion applies to multi-step authentication and recovery. [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) [W3C accessible-authentication explanation](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/accessible-authentication-minimum.html)

Requirements by exclusion case:

| User constraint | Required launch path |
|---|---|
| No smartphone | Desktop authenticator app, platform/roaming passkey or hardware key, printed recovery code, and accessible human appeal. WhatsApp cannot be required. |
| No phone number | TOTP/passkey + email verification/recovery address + offline codes. Registration and private use MUST remain possible. |
| No government ID | Normal service and recovery through pre-bound authenticators/codes/contacts; ID cannot be a universal gate. Lost-everything may remain unrecoverable if no pre-bound proof exists, and that consequence MUST be disclosed before enrollment. |
| Shared device/phone | Private-browsing guidance, no “trust this device” by default, explicit sign-out, named sessions, offline code or separate hardware/passkey option, and no prohibition on a physical device being registered to multiple users solely for fraud convenience. NIST advises public-facing services not to prevent shared-device registration, subject to abuse controls. [NIST authenticators](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b/authenticators/) |
| Blocked/unavailable messaging app | Same-screen app-independent fallback; no hidden dependency on app store, push notification, roaming, or carrier. |
| Cognitive/learning disability | Autofill and password-manager support, paste allowed in password/OTP/code fields, clear time remaining, one task per screen, no memory trivia/CAPTCHA-only path, passkey option, and an assisted human channel. |
| Blind/low vision or motor disability | WCAG 2.2 AA semantics/focus/error messaging; QR MUST have manual key; no drag/gesture-only step; screen-reader-labeled code groups; sufficient time and extension; webcam/biometric alternative. |
| Language/literacy limitation | Plain localized instructions, icons plus text, no security-critical meaning by color alone, human interpreter route with privacy safeguards, and no assumption that legal name matches Latin-script account data. |
| Lost or inaccessible camera/biometric | TOTP/passkey/security key/recovery code path; a biometric capture failure is not account failure. |

Cross-cutting requirements:

- MUST meet WCAG 2.2 AA for registration, MFA enrollment, sign-in, recovery, security notices, support handoff, and cancellation—not only the normal login page.
- MUST allow password managers, operating-system credential managers, paste, autofill, and accessible QR/manual alternatives; MUST NOT block them as “fraud.”
- MUST test with users of assistive technology and users without a smartphone/phone number before private launch. Target completion/error parity is **UNVERIFIED until research**; release criteria MUST be set from that testing, not guessed.
- MUST provide support through at least one non-voice and one human-accessible route. Support may assist navigation/communication but cannot lower proof requirements. Reasonable accommodation MUST change the interface/evidence collection method, not turn an untrusted conversation into authentication.
- MUST display expected wait, required evidence categories, current state, case ID, and appeal/cancel options; MUST avoid repeated collection when a user switches channel; and MUST preserve translation/accessibility preferences in the handoff without sensitive chat history.

**Recommendation — confidence: high.** Make a no-phone, no-ID, accessible recovery path a launch acceptance criterion, while transparently preserving fail-closed ownership rules. **Strongest counter-argument:** providing feature-equivalent alternatives across all constraints is expensive and may weaken proof independence; staged launch countries and a narrower supported-device baseline could deliver a safer initial service sooner.

## D. Support Bot A, support Bot B, and human boundaries

### D1 — Bot A exact allowlist, denylist, and structural enforcement

Bot A is an untrusted conversational principal. It may make only low-risk, reversible support actions and may not authenticate a user or touch security-sensitive state. Prompt instructions are not an authorization boundary. OWASP recommends deny-by-default, permission validation on every request, least-privilege tools, human approval for high-risk operations, and treating external content as untrusted. [OWASP authorization](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authorization_Cheat_Sheet.html) [OWASP AI-agent security](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/AI_Agent_Security_Cheat_Sheet.html)

Exact launch allowlist:

| Allowed action | Preconditions and maximum effect |
|---|---|
| Search/read approved public help and status content | Read-only from a versioned support knowledge corpus; no retrieval of user/debate records, internal runbooks, risk rules, or unpublished incident data. |
| Change chat-only language, font/contrast preference, or accessibility presentation | Applies only to the current support-chat UI; must not mutate the account profile or security settings. |
| Create a support case | Uses a server-supplied opaque session subject if one exists, or an unauthenticated contact supplied for reply. Collect category, language/accessibility need, and user-authored description with explicit consent. No inferred ownership or authenticated state results. |
| Append the current user's message/attachment reference to that case | Content is size/type-limited, malware-scanned where applicable, labeled untrusted, and may not be fetched/rendered by the bot. No ID documents, biometrics, credentials, or secrets are accepted. |
| Read case number, coarse queue state, and public ETA for the current case | Subject/case authorization is enforced by the server session or one-time case-view capability, not by identifiers the model supplies. No transcript, evidence, staff notes, other-user data, or risk score. |
| Close, reopen, or withdraw the current support case | Reversible case workflow only; does not delete audit, evidence, account, debate, or security state. Must show confirmation and result. |
| Create a bug, accessibility, abuse, or suspected-takeover report | Creates a report only. It cannot decide abuse, freeze/unfreeze an account, reveal account existence, alter access, or initiate recovery. The UI may offer a separate deterministic protective-freeze control. |

Everything not listed is denied. Explicitly denied:

- Authenticate/verify a user; ask for or inspect passwords, OTPs, TOTP seeds, recovery codes, passkeys, session cookies, API keys, provider tokens, ID documents, selfies, biometrics, or full payment data.
- Start, approve, accelerate, cancel, or complete account recovery; reveal whether an account exists; enumerate/mask its email, phone, apps, factors, passkeys, sessions, devices, roles, risk score, recovery status, or audit events.
- Add/remove/change any password, factor, email, phone, messaging address, recovery contact, session, owner, role, permission, tenant/user/debate link, or staff access.
- Read/write private debate content; publish/unpublish/export/delete/crypto-shred a debate or account; invoke debate providers; change model/provider selection; spend money; issue refunds; or act in an admin console.
- Execute SQL, shell, browser, webhook, arbitrary URL fetch, file-system access, email/message send, infrastructure/cloud action, or generic HTTP request; call an unlisted tool; or use an arbitrary object identifier.

Structural enforcement requirements:

- MUST give Bot A a dedicated service identity with only the seven allowlisted capabilities; no wildcard permission, database credential, auth-provider credential, cloud credential, or administrator token may exist in its runtime.
- MUST put every action behind a deterministic policy gateway that derives subject, tenant, case, allowed transition, and current authorization from trusted server context. The model may propose a typed action but cannot supply or override the security context. Every request is checked; unmatched, malformed, stale, cross-user, cross-tenant, or out-of-order requests fail closed. [OWASP Authorization Cheat Sheet](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Authorization_Cheat_Sheet.html)
- MUST restrict network routes to the approved knowledge service and support-case gateway; deny DNS/private-network/metadata/arbitrary internet access; and validate every request/response against a closed, versioned set of allowed fields and types, plus size, count, timeout, retry, and idempotency limits.
- MUST show the exact proposed reversible effect to the user and require confirmation for create/append/close/reopen actions. Tool success, failure, and actual affected object come from the gateway, not model narration.
- MUST audit model version, policy version, case, proposed action, authorization decision, result, and human escalation without logging secret content. MUST have a kill switch independent of the model.
- MUST have authorization regression tests for every allowed action crossed with anonymous/user/staff/other-user/other-tenant/stale-case contexts, plus a negative test proving every denied domain is unreachable. Testing is defensive and uses fixtures/synthetic accounts only.

**Recommendation — confidence: high.** Treat Bot A as a case-navigation UI with six capabilities, not as an account agent. **Strongest counter-argument:** such a narrow bot resolves little and may increase human ticket volume; carefully allowing session revocation or a protective freeze could reduce harm while remaining reversible.

### D2 — Deterministic “cannot solve securely” handoff

Bot A MUST hand off rather than improvise when any of these predicates is true:

1. The user's requested action is not one of D1's seven exact allowlisted actions.
2. The request asks about identity, account existence/ownership, credentials, MFA, recovery, contact/factor/session/role changes, private data, deletion/export/publication, provider or admin state.
3. Required subject/case scope is missing, expired, ambiguous, conflicting, or belongs to another user/tenant.
4. The operation is irreversible, externally visible, high impact, financially consequential, legally sensitive, or may reveal another person's/private debate data.
5. The user reports takeover, device/email/phone compromise, lost all factors, coercion, stalking, minor/vulnerable-person concerns, security incident, privacy-rights request, or law-enforcement/legal demand.
6. Two allowed attempts fail, provider/system state conflicts with the bot's explanation, the bot lacks a current source, its calibrated answer confidence is below the approved support threshold, or it detects prompt/tool manipulation. “Two attempts” and the confidence threshold are engineering controls whose optimal values are **UNVERIFIED**.
7. The user asks for a human, requests an accessibility accommodation the bot cannot provide, or cannot understand/complete the flow.

Handoff package requirements:

- MUST include only: case ID; server-supplied opaque subject if authenticated; claimed contact channel separately labeled unverified; locale/accessibility preference; category and concise user intent; allowed actions already attempted and their typed results; timestamps; escalation reason; and the minimum sanitized transcript excerpts the user can review and consent to transfer.
- MUST exclude credentials/secrets, TOTP QR/seed, OTP/recovery codes, session/token values, raw ID/biometric material, unnecessary debate text, other-user data, internal risk thresholds, model hidden reasoning, and speculative identity findings.
- MUST clearly tell the user what could not be done, that handoff does not authenticate them, expected queue/next step, case ID, data being transferred, and how to add/correct information. The human MUST NOT force the user to repeat non-secret context already present.
- MUST provide a separate deterministic link for urgent protective freeze/reporting. The bot itself cannot freeze because an unauthenticated attacker could weaponize it for denial of service.
- MUST log the predicate that caused handoff and measure unsafe self-resolution attempts, repeat explanations, abandoned handoffs, time to human response, and human reclassification without storing prohibited content.

**Recommendation — confidence: high on the predicates and data minimization, medium on the two-attempt trigger.** Escalate on policy uncertainty, not only model uncertainty. **Strongest counter-argument:** broad mandatory escalation can overwhelm a small launch team and frustrate users; bounded scripts for common factor-loss cases could safely collect more before a human joins.

### D3 — Bot B one-way evidence system, no-egress isolation, and console safety

Bot B may ask higher-privilege identity/recovery questions, but it is an evidence collector only. It cannot authenticate, grant recovery, change state, browse/fetch, or disclose its findings to the claimant. User input is untrusted even when it appears in a “trusted” account record. OWASP notes that natural-language instructions and data share a channel, recommends least privilege and human oversight, and warns that model guardrails are another fallible layer rather than a deterministic boundary. [OWASP prompt-injection prevention](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/LLM_Prompt_Injection_Prevention_Cheat_Sheet.html)

Required dataflow contract:

1. A human opens a scoped recovery case and chooses an approved evidence questionnaire. A deterministic broker sends Bot B only the case pseudonym, locale/accessibility setting, question-state IDs, and claimant responses.
2. The only claimant-facing output Bot B may cause is selection of an ID from a finite, versioned, human-approved question/clarification catalog plus non-sensitive progress/refusal codes. The broker renders the actual text. Free-form model findings, explanations, links, files, markup, or tool output can never traverse this route.
3. Claimant answers flow inward, are size/type limited and labeled untrusted, and are appended with provenance and timestamps. Bot B may emit one fixed, versioned finding record to a one-way evidence sink containing only: question ID, raw-answer reference, normalized non-secret features, consistency/conflict flags, confidence range, and citations to case evidence. It cannot read staff notes or previously accumulated human decisions back from the sink.
4. Only human reviewers may view the evidence/finding. A separate deterministic recovery service—not Bot B or the console—enforces proof minimums, delays, revocations, and approval separation.

No-egress isolation requirements:

- MUST run one ephemeral case environment with no general network route, DNS, cloud metadata, private network, internet, email/message provider, browser, shell, database, filesystem outside its ephemeral volume, clipboard, extension, webhook, analytics, crash-upload, or reusable credential.
- The only allowed interfaces are the narrowly authenticated inbound dialogue broker, question-ID return, health signal, and append-only typed evidence sink. Network policy/hypervisor controls—not prompts—MUST enforce direction, destination, permitted fields/types, byte/record budget, and case binding. No endpoint may accept an arbitrary URL or return fetched content.
- The model and required dependencies MUST run locally/self-hosted within the isolated boundary. A remote third-party LLM call would be egress and is prohibited for Bot B under V's fixed rule. If an adequate local model cannot meet accuracy/accessibility requirements, Bot B MUST be deferred and humans must run the questionnaire.
- The case environment MUST be destroyed after sealed handoff; secrets MUST never be present; all artifacts MUST carry case/user provenance; and cross-case memory/caching is prohibited.

Poisoning and safe-console requirements:

- MUST preserve claimant content as an untrusted allegation, never silently promote it to verified fact, instruction, policy, role, or score. Derived fields MUST link to the source, algorithm/model/prompt version, and transformation. A human must corroborate any identity-relevant fact with independent pre-bound evidence.
- MUST render raw and derived untrusted content as inert text with context-appropriate output encoding; disable HTML/Markdown execution, active links, previews, images, remote fonts, SVG, iframes, scripts, event handlers, copy-triggered commands, and automatic URL fetch. Use restrictive CSP/Trusted Types as defense in depth, not the primary control. [OWASP XSS prevention](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/Cross_Site_Scripting_Prevention_Cheat_Sheet.html)
- MUST keep untrusted evidence out of staff-agent/system prompts and tool-capable assistants. If a later human-facing summarizer is used, it MUST have no tools/credentials, receive typed fields rather than executable markup, label quotations, and require a reviewer to open original inert evidence before decision.
- MUST bound response length, attachment type/count, questions, retries, compute/time, and case lifetime; scan permitted attachments outside the console's trust domain; and support a security kill switch.
- MUST test with synthetic prompt-injection/markup/URL/Unicode/oversize fixtures to prove that no network request, code/markup execution, tool call, cross-case read, claimant-facing finding, or authorization change occurs. This is defensive validation, not offensive testing against third parties.

**Recommendation — confidence: high on the isolation and typed one-way boundary, medium on whether a local model can provide worthwhile evidence quality.** Implement Bot B only if the no-egress and inert-console properties are independently testable. **Strongest counter-argument:** a finite question catalog and local model remove most value of an LLM; a conventional form with human review may be safer, cheaper, and easier to audit.

### D4 — Proving chat grants no greater access than no chat

The security invariant is:

> For the same pre-chat authenticated session, bound authenticators, and deterministic recovery state, any sequence of Bot A or Bot B messages can leave the user's effective authentication/authorization/recovery entitlements unchanged. Chat may create evidence/cases only; it cannot increase access.

Requirements:

- MUST represent chat/case/evidence state in a domain that is not accepted as an authenticator, session, recovery code, factor, contact, role, approval, or authorization input. No “verified by bot,” confidence score, sentiment, conversational consistency, or staff note can satisfy a proof predicate.
- MUST ensure Bot A has no auth/recovery capabilities and Bot B has only the D3 dialogue/evidence interfaces. Human reviewers must use the same deterministic recovery service and cannot create a bypass token, waive the minimum delay, or mark a proof successful by free-text discretion.
- MUST derive account/tenant/object scope from authenticated server state; an identifier, email, phone, username, debate detail, or link supplied in chat is always a claim. Unauthenticated chat responses MUST not reveal account existence, masked contacts, enrolled factors, user/debate membership, case ownership, risk band, or whether a guessed fact is correct.
- MAY allow any person to submit a takeover report or request a protective hold through a separate abuse-resistant mechanism, because that only reduces access. Such a hold MUST be rate-limited, contestable, reversible with normal strong proof, and unavailable as an account-existence oracle.
- MUST enforce separation of duties for high-risk recovery: one reviewer collects/assesses; a different passkey-authenticated reviewer approves where manual override is permitted. The system policy—not either reviewer—applies proof minimums and holds. Staff cannot approve their own/related account or a case they initiated.
- MUST run state-invariant tests that replay arbitrary/synthetic chat transcripts and assert zero changes to credentials, sessions, contacts, roles, debate visibility, recovery entitlement, or proof state; only case/evidence/audit rows may differ. Include cross-user/tenant and concurrent recovery cases.

**Recommendation — confidence: high.** Make the invariant a release-blocking property test and remove discretionary support overrides. **Strongest counter-argument:** strict non-discretion can fail legitimate edge cases where policy data is wrong or accessibility prevents standard proof; a tightly governed executive/security exception may be necessary, but it would violate the requested “no greater access” rule and must be a V-level change.

- Authentication, recovery, support, and evidence stores MUST be purpose-separated. Search/analytics/debate-generation indexes and LLM training/evaluation sets MUST exclude support and recovery content. Access and audit requirements:

- Staff MUST use named, phishing-resistant accounts; least-privilege role, just-in-time case access, reason entry, time limit, and dual approval for recovery; no shared accounts, bulk browsing, unrestricted search, or production database access for ordinary support.

- MUST alert on bulk access, cross-tenant queries, after-hours anomalies, repeated failed proof, self/related-account access, export, policy override attempts, and Bot A/B boundary denial. Exact thresholds are **UNVERIFIED until operational baselines**.
- User crypto-shredding MUST cover private support/recovery content and derived indexes through per-user/per-case key destruction while preserving only legally justified non-content audit. Public debate artifacts remain under the separate V ruling and MUST never contain auth/recovery evidence. Backups MUST honor the same key-destruction boundary and documented expiry.
- Security/legal hold MUST be scoped, approved, visible to authorized privacy/security staff, time-limited/reviewed, and must not become indefinite default retention. Deletion requests MUST show what was deleted, retained, why, and expected backup/key expiry without exposing attacker-useful detail.

**Recommendation — confidence: high on minimization/access/audit, medium on the numerical retention caps.** Adopt the proposed caps as launch defaults and require counsel to approve any extension. **Strongest counter-argument:** 30/90-day evidence windows may be too short for delayed takeover discovery, litigation, or abuse-pattern analysis; longer retention can improve investigations but increases breach and privacy harm.

### D6 — Cases that always require a human or refusal of AI involvement

AI MAY perform minimal routing and accessible form assistance, but MUST NOT investigate, adjudicate, approve, or execute these cases:

- Any claim of account ownership, lost factor/recovery, suspected takeover, compromised email/messaging/device, lost-everything, contested recovery, or reversal of a security change.
- Adding/removing/replacing a password, passkey, TOTP, recovery code set, email, phone/app address, recovery contact, session, role, ownership, or tenant link.
- Review of government ID, selfie, liveness, biometric match, legal-name mismatch, minor/guardian claim, deceased/incapacitated user, coercion/stalking/domestic-abuse risk, or accessibility accommodation that changes evidence collection.
- Privacy/data-subject request, private-data export/deletion/crypto-shredding, deliberate public publication/unpublication, legal hold, subpoena/law-enforcement request, regulator complaint, or cross-border restriction.
- Security incident/breach, insider/staff abuse, bot boundary failure, provider compromise, mass recovery anomaly, sanctions/export-control issue, or a case involving a staff/administrator/high-risk public figure.
- Appeal of an automated risk decision, suspected bias/false match, ambiguous identity collision, conflicting verified channels, or any policy exception.

Bot B MAY collect pre-approved answers after a human opens such a case, but the human must review original evidence and a separate authorized human must approve high-risk recovery. AI MUST be refused entirely—not merely supervised—when the case requires exposure of raw ID/biometric material to a model, secret/credential inspection, legal advice/decision, law-enforcement disclosure, safety-critical counseling, or when D3 isolation is unavailable.

Human involvement does not mean discretion to lower proof. Humans MUST use the same proof vocabulary, reason codes, holds, notification, appeal, separation-of-duty, and audit requirements; they may resolve data quality, choose an allowed accessible method, and deny/approve only within policy.

**Recommendation — confidence: high.** Reserve every ownership/security/legal decision for policy-bound humans and refuse model processing of raw identity/secret evidence. **Strongest counter-argument:** two-person review and broad human routing may not scale at 100k users; validated automation could eventually handle low-risk appeals, but only after measured error, legal, and boundary evidence exists.
