# Breach clocks: who we tell, and how fast

Date: 1 October 2026. One line per country we open beyond the EU. Not reviewed by counsel; [unverified] marks a
point not read in the primary text.

| Country | Tell | When | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| EU/EEA (lead: Romania) | ANSPDCP; the people affected when the risk is high | 72 hours from becoming aware, where the breach is likely to put people at risk | GDPR Art. 33–34 |
| Switzerland | FDPIC (through its "DataBreach" portal) | "as quickly as possible", when the breach is likely to lead to a high risk to the people concerned; no fixed hours. Document every breach and keep the record 2 years | FADP Art. 24; DPO Art. 15(4); FDPIC data-breach guidelines v1.2 (23 April 2025) |
| Moldova | CNPDCP | within 72 hours of becoming aware, unless the breach is unlikely to put people at risk; tell the people affected when the risk is high. In force since 23 August 2026 (fines phase in: 10% in year one, 40% in year two) | Law No. 195/2024 Art. 33–34, 89(1), 90(4) (CNPDCP English translation; the Romanian text on legis.md was not read [unverified]) |
| Israel | Privacy Protection Authority (the Registrar) | "immediately", for a "severe security incident" in a medium- or high-level database; also report the steps taken. Ours is medium level (political and religious views), or basic if no more than 10 people can reach it; a basic-level database only logs incidents. The 24–72-hour figure in PPA guidance is [unverified] | Protection of Privacy (Data Security) Regulations 5777-2017, reg. 1, 11(a), 11(d) and First Schedule |
| Taiwan | Today: the people affected, "in an appropriate manner", once the facts are known. Not yet: the PDPC | The amendment promulgated 11 November 2025 adds a duty to report to the PDPC (amended Art. 12(2)), but its start date is not set (MOJ database, September 2026: "final effective date: not set"); the PDPC will fix the scope and time limit | PDPA Art. 12 (version of 31 May 2023); amendment of 11 Nov 2025, https://www.pdpc.gov.tw/News_Content/20/1010/ |
