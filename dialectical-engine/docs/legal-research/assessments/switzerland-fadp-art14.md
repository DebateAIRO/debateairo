# Switzerland: do we need a representative? (FADP Art. 14)

Date: 1 October 2026. Next review: 1 October 2027, or earlier on a trigger below.
Controller: DebateAIRO S.R.L., Bucharest, Romania. No establishment in Switzerland.
Source: Federal Act on Data Protection, SR 235.1, Art. 14–15 (https://www.fedlex.admin.ch/eli/cc/2022/491/en,
English text checked against the German). Not reviewed by counsel.

## The rule

A controller abroad must name a representative in Switzerland only if ALL FOUR hold (Art. 14(1)):

| | Condition | Us at launch |
|---|---|---|
| a | the processing is connected with offering goods or services to people in Switzerland (or monitoring them) | **Met.** Sign-up is open to people in Switzerland. |
| b | the processing is on a large scale (*umfangreich*) | **Not met at launch.** A new service with launch-stage Swiss user numbers. |
| c | the processing is regular (*regelmässig*) | **Met.** The service runs all the time. |
| d | the processing poses a high risk to the personality of the people concerned | **Arguably met.** Debates may reveal political or religious views, which are sensitive data (Art. 5(c)(1)). |

The exemption rests on (b) alone. Neither the Act nor the FDPIC gives a number for "large scale".

## Conclusion

We do not need a representative in Switzerland at launch, because the processing is not on a large scale.
The Privacy Policy (Annex B.6) says: "We have assessed that we do not need a representative in Switzerland
(Art. 14 FADP). We review this every year."

## Review triggers (any one reopens this file before the yearly date)

- the number of Swiss accounts grows enough that the processing could be called large scale;
- sensitive data becomes central to a feature rather than incidental to a debate;
- the FDPIC publishes guidance or a figure for "large scale".

If the answer changes: appoint a representative, publish their name and address (Art. 14(3)), and give them the
record of processing activities they must keep (Art. 15).

## Related duties that do not wait for a representative

- Breach: report to the FDPIC "as quickly as possible" when a breach is likely to lead to a high risk
  (Art. 24); document every breach and keep the record 2 years (DPO Art. 15(4)). See `breach-clocks.md`.
- Record of processing activities (Art. 12): firms under 250 staff are exempt (DPO Art. 24) unless they process
  a large volume of sensitive data; keep one anyway.
- Large-scale processing of sensitive data would also call for a data protection impact assessment (Art. 22).
- No registration with the FDPIC: only federal bodies notify their records (Art. 12(4)).
