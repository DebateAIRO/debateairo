# Moldova: do we need a representative? (Law No. 195/2024, Art. 27)

Date: 1 October 2026. Next review: 1 October 2027, or earlier if the CNPDCP rules on the point.
Controller: DebateAIRO S.R.L., Bucharest, Romania. No establishment in Moldova.
Source: Law No. 195/2024 on personal data protection, Official Gazette No. 367-369, art. 574, 23 August 2024,
read in the CNPDCP's own English translation
(https://datepersonale.md/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Law-no.-195-2024-on-personal-data-protection-1.pdf).
The binding Romanian text on legis.md was not read (a bot check blocked it) [unverified]. Not reviewed by counsel.

## Does the law reach us?

Yes. Art. 3(2) covers controllers not established in Moldova that offer goods or services to people in Moldova,
"irrespective of whether a payment … is required". A free sign-up is enough. The law has been in force since
23 August 2026 (Art. 89(1): 24 months after publication); only the fines phase in (Art. 90(4)).

## The rule

Art. 27(1): a controller not established in Moldova that the law reaches must designate in writing a
representative established in Moldova.

Art. 27(2) exempts three cases. The one that covers us is (c): controllers and processors established in a
European Economic Area member state.

## Conclusion

We do not need a representative in Moldova: DebateAIRO S.R.L. is established in Romania, an EEA member state.
The Privacy Policy (Annex B.7) says so and cites Art. 27(2)(c).

We do not rely on the GDPR-style exemption in Art. 27(2)(a) (occasional processing, no large-scale special-category
data, unlikely to result in risk). It would be doubtful for us: sign-ups are systematic, and debates may reveal
political or religious views (Art. 9(1)).

Open doubts: the Romanian text is unread, and we found no CNPDCP decision applying the EEA exemption.

## Related duties

- Breach: tell the CNPDCP within 72 hours (Art. 33); tell the people affected when the risk is high (Art. 34).
  See `breach-clocks.md`.
- Transfers: EEA hosting is outside the transfer rules (Art. 44(2)). Onward transfers to US AI providers rest on
  standard clauses "approved by the Centre or adopted by the European Commission" (Art. 46(2)(c)), or on the
  CNPDCP's adequacy list (Art. 45; the list was not found [unverified]). The Register names the mechanism per provider.
- Data protection officer: required for large-scale processing of special-category data (Art. 37(1)(c), the GDPR
  test). If we ever appoint one, send their contact details to the CNPDCP (Art. 37(7)).
- No registration with the CNPDCP; the old register went with Law No. 133/2011 (repealed, Art. 90(3)(b)).
  Records of processing are kept internally (Art. 30).
