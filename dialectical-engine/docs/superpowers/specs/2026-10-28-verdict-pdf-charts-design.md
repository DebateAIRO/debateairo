# Charts in the verdict PDF — design (2026-10-28)

## Intent

The owner's downloadable report (`GET /debate/{id}/report`) is text only. Where a statement in the story compares
things the debate put numbers on (prices, benchmark scores, the strength of competing positions), a chart drawn right
under that statement makes the comparison visible. The owner's example is a cost-vs-score scatter of AI models
(Artificial Analysis' "Intelligence Index vs. Cost per Task"). Charts appear **only where they make a difference**:
when two measures cover the same items, a scatter; one measure across several items, a bar chart; otherwise nothing.

## Owner decisions (2026-10-28)

- Charts may use **figures the AI models found and cited in their arguments**, labelled as not independently
  verified, **plus the engine's own scores** (strengths).
- The charts are **AI-generated**: an AI model chooses where a chart helps, what it compares and how it is labelled.
- Under every chart: the AI-generated note and **"Copyrighted by DMS Merchandise Shop SRL"** (fixed text, every language).
- The Terms of Service gain a clause that DMS Merchandise Shop SRL owns the charts and statistics it generates.
- A logo goes in the chart's corner once the company has one; until then a text wordmark.

## Calls made by the builder (not decided by the owner)

1. **AI chooses, code draws.** The model never draws; it returns a chart plan as JSON. Code validates every number and
   draws the chart in vector form with react-pdf `<Svg>`. A model cannot add a value the debate never contained.
2. **A separate chart call after the story is accepted** — `STORY:CHARTS:1`, role SYNTHESIZER, story lane, contract
   `story.charts.v1`, sent to the maker that wrote the served story (with the run's cost fallback, as round 1).
   The storyteller and checker contracts stay byte for byte. Any failure (money, parse, provider) means **no charts**;
   the story's outcome never depends on its charts.
3. **Number check.** A `figure` value carries `text` copied from the cited point's claim. Code keeps the chart only when
   that text appears in the claim (whitespace-normalised) and the number it states equals `value` (either decimal
   convention). `strength` values are never taken from the model: the PDF reads the point's final strength (else its
   base strength) from the answer itself, the same numbers the appendix prints. A chart with any failing item is dropped
   whole, never repaired.
4. **Stored once, drawn on every download.** Valid charts ride in the story's sealed content as an optional `charts`
   member (no migration, as R1d's `backup`), are served on `GET /v1/answers/{id}/story` as an optional `charts` field,
   and are owner-only like the long story. Every download shows the same charts; no AI call at download time.
5. **Placement.** A chart is anchored after one paragraph of "Why this answer" (`why.N`) or of the long story
   (`long.S.P`), 1-based. Chart and caption sit in one unbreakable block, kept together on a page.
6. **Limits.** At most 4 charts per story; 2–12 items per chart (scatter: at least 3); labels ≤ 80 characters,
   titles ≤ 160. A log scale only when every value is above zero.
7. **Caption.** Title above the plot; below: "From points P3, P5 and P9" (code-built from the items' references),
   "Chart generated by AI from the debate's arguments.", "Cited figures were not independently verified." (when a
   figure axis is present), "Strengths were calculated by Dialectical Engine." (when a strength axis is present), and
   the copyright line. "About this report" gains one sentence on how the charts were made, only when there are charts.
8. **Corner mark.** `apps/ui/assets/brand/report-logo.png` when the file exists, otherwise the "Dialectical Engine"
   wordmark in the report's muted grey. Dropping the file in is all a logo needs.
9. **Right-to-left reports** mirror the caption alignment and the corner mark; plot coordinates stay left to right.

## Non-goals

- No chart on the public page or the site's story panel (owner-only PDF, like the long story).
- No charts for stories written before this change, and no re-writing of old stories.
- No AI-drawn images, no charts at download time.
- No change to the storyteller's or checker's wording, rounds or money.

## Data blocks: tables, and which debates get blocks (2026-10-30)

The first real debate (three phones compared on security) got no chart: the chart call ran and the model, told that
"most reports need no chart", returned an empty list. The owner asked for charts on the debates that need them, tables
beside them, and none on the debates that do not.

**Owner decisions (2026-10-30)**

- Q1. Seventeen kinds of question. Nine compare things: pick among named options, yes/no decision, is it true, how much,
  best way, money trade-off, why/cause, prediction, trend. Eight are answered in words and get no block: values,
  personal, taste, interpretation, definition, hypothetical, a private person, a single fact. Crisis stays with the
  question check.
- Q2. First release: the comparison table, the ranking table, and pros and cons, beside the existing bar and scatter.
- Q3. A table cell or entry is copied word for word from the claim it cites. One that fails the check is emptied
  ("not stated in the debate"); a table is left out only when too little is left of it.
- Q4. Health and legal questions get tables and charts like any other; the cover's "not professional advice" line
  stands for them.
- Q5. Three answers or more always get the ranking of the answers, in the list of points for a kind answered in words.
- Q6. A debate with nothing worth showing gets no block call at all.
- Q7. Nothing is stored or logged about why a story got no block.
- Q9. The block call names the kind of question (an enum in its reply).
- Q10. No copyright line under tables, for now (Terms 2.9 names charts only); to be settled later.
- Q11. PDF only, as the charts.

**How it works**

1. **The need test** (code, `storyBlockNeeds`): figures (three claims or more state numbers), compare (two options at
   least, argued with two points or more), pros and cons (a position argued for and against, or two positions argued),
   ranking (three positions or more). The block call (`story.charts.v2`, still `STORY:CHARTS:1`) runs only when one of
   the first three holds.
2. **The block call** gets the same material plus a `figures` field (each claim's figures as code read them) and
   returns the kind of question, charts and tables. Code keeps only the blocks the kind's menu allows
   (`packages/story/src/forms.ts`); an unknown kind keeps every kind, and the checks alone decide.
3. **The ranking** is code's (`storyRanking`): each position by place, with whether it won and whether it finished
   within the tie margin. The PDF reads each claim and strength from the answer.
4. **Storage and serving**: an optional `tables` member beside `charts`, sealed and owner-only the same way.
5. **The PDF**: a table prints after its paragraph, before that paragraph's chart; the ranking prints at the end of
   "Why this answer" or at the head of the list of points. Cells link to their points. About gains one sentence when
   the report has tables. Eleven new strings in 35 locales.

## Chart relevance (2026-11-16)

The first chart a real debate printed was "Point strength for claims about coding value": three bars, 98%, 98% and
96.2%, for three points that said the same thing, under a question about cost per task and benchmark score. The debate
stated no figures (its models would not invent prices), so the only measure left to the plan was a strength, and a
strength was offered for any point. Nothing in code refused it: the need test had already found no figures, but the
call still ran for the tables, and every check a chart met was about figures. The owner asked for the charts to show
relevant data.

**What a chart must now be** (builder's calls; the reply's form and the contract id `story.charts.v2` are unchanged)

1. **A strength compares the answers.** On a chart with a strength axis every item is a position, each once
   (`CHART_STRENGTH_NOT_ANSWER`). This is the intent's own wording, "the strength of competing positions". The plan's
   check holds a new story to it. The report holds every stored story to it too, by the rule its list of points names a
   position by (a point that argues about nothing else, `storyPositionIds`), so a chart stored before this change is
   no longer printed.
2. **One axis, one unit.** Two figures written with different currency or percent signs never share an axis
   (`CHART_FIGURE_UNITS_DIFFER`): a price beside a percentage, dollars beside euros. A unit written in words is not
   compared, because words inflect ("1 an", "5 ani") and one debate writes "12%" and "8 percent".
3. **An axis compares something.** Figures that all state the same value are no chart (`CHART_NOTHING_TO_COMPARE`).
4. **A scale word is read.** The number check wanted `value` equal to the number as written, so "$1.2 million" passed
   only as 1.2 and was drawn beside "$800,000" at a millionth of its size; the correct 1,200,000 was refused. Where a
   letter follows the number, `value` may be the written number times a power of ten from a thousand to a trillion.
   Code reads no scale word of any language.
5. **A name's number is not a figure.** `claimFigures` read "iPhone 14", "Galaxy S24" and "Reno 12" as the figures 14,
   24 and 12: they opened the need test and were handed to the call as numbers to chart. A number glued to a letter
   (S24, GPT-5, COVID-19), or following a name (iPhone 14, Windows 11), is left out.
6. **The call is told all of it**: what a strength is charted for, that a name's number is no figure, one unit per
   axis, and that `value` is the whole number a figure states.

| Piece | Where |
|---|---|
| The strength rule, one unit per axis, a scale word, the wording | `packages/story/src/charts.ts` |
| A name's number; the positions the charts are checked against (`sides`) | `packages/story/src/blocks.ts` |
| The report's own strength rule | `apps/ui/lib/report/reportCharts.ts`, `apps/ui/lib/v3/storyWords.ts` (`storyPositionIds`) |

## Known limits

- A debate that states no figures can still get one chart: the strengths of its answers side by side. With three
  answers or more the ranking table already prints the same numbers.
- Units written in words are not compared, so "7 years" beside "3 auditors" on one axis rests on the call's wording.
- A plan may still give 1.2 for "$1.2 million" beside 800,000 for "$800,000": both state their text. The wording asks
  for the whole number; code cannot tell which the model meant.
- `claimFigures` reads names by form. In a language that capitalises every noun (German, "pro Jahr 500 Euro") it leaves
  a real figure out of the need test and the `figures` field. It is never a check: a chart's figures are checked against
  the claim itself.

- The chart call has no length-guidance step and no thinking-level row of its own: on the private preview it is a
  STANDARD call (`previewCallTierOf`), with the storyteller's bound.
- Right-to-left reports mirror the title row only; the plot's axes run left to right.
- Label widths are estimated (no text measuring before layout): a very long label is cut with an ellipsis.
