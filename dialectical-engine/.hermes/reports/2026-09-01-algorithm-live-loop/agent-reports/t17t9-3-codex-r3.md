## Q3 — The +0.43/+0.12-second hint and a useful sample

The arithmetic is right for the **registration API maxima**: +429 ms and +115 ms; TIP differs by 326 ms between its two conditions, while PARENT differs by 12 ms. The resend maxima differ TIP-minus-PARENT by +134 ms and +53 ms. These are not measured queue-wait effects.

I agree that the present data cannot distinguish a code effect from environmental variation. But 326 ms is a range across two differently prepared runs, not an estimate of within-condition noise or a standard deviation; no significance or equivalence claim follows. Both pairings ran TIP first. TIP lived in the synced worktree and PARENT in `/private/tmp`, as their subject logs show at line 2, so storage placement is another systematic confound.

A useful new sample would consist of independent **paired subject runs**, balanced/randomized in tree order, with both trees on comparable storage, a verified sustained load condition, a subject-only control condition, and actual identity-linked queue timing. Analyze route-specific per-run outcomes and queue waits; the 32 markers in one shared queue are not 32 independent experimental runs. A practical pilot could use 10–20 pairs per condition to estimate paired variability, followed by a predeclared effect size and precision/power target. That pilot size is not a guarantee of discrimination.

There is no defensible final sample size in this package. For scale only: under a normal paired-mean approximation, two-sided 5% significance and 80% power give `n ≈ (2.8 × σ_pair / δ)²`. **If** paired SD were 0.33 s and the target effect 0.12 s, this would suggest about 60 pairs; 0.33 s has not been estimated as an SD here, and run maxima/tail failures require their own analysis. At roughly 2.4 minutes per existing subject, 60 pairs already need about five hours of subject execution alone.

The records show that the chosen burner configurations were costly and prevented entry, not that matched sampling on this hardware is impossible. They also do not prove OneDrive cannot be controlled. More samples without correcting storage/order/load and event definitions would not settle attribution. The 20:46 authorization to 22:28 filing spans about 102 minutes: the ~3-hour cap was respected, but not exhausted. No sufficiently powered experiment is established as achievable within the remaining time, and I do not authorize another round for V.
