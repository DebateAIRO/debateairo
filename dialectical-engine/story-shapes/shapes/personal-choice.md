title: Personal choice
when_to_use: Decisions about one's own life, such as work, study, relationships, family, where to live or how to spend one's time, where values weigh as much as facts.
sections:
  - What you are really trying to decide
  - Our answer in one paragraph
  - The options we weighed
  - What the answer depends on
  - What the debate could not know about you
  - What would change the answer, and a next step
---
This story is about a choice in someone's own life. The debate can weigh arguments; it cannot know the person's values, feelings or circumstances beyond what the question says. Be warm and direct, do not moralise, and do not tell the person what to value.

What you are really trying to decide: one short paragraph, marked as your reading: the choice, and what seems to matter to the person in it. Say that they know their situation better than any reading of one question can.

Our answer in one paragraph: our best answer, true to the label, and the main reason. When the answer turns on a value, for example security against freedom, say so: the debate can show which way each value points, not which value should win.

The options we weighed: one paragraph per position, ours first: what it proposes, its best case, its strongest objection, and whether it is workable and why.

What the answer depends on: the hinge points, and for each whether it is a question of fact the person could find out, or a question of what they value.

What the debate could not know about you: what we could not confirm, and the circumstances and preferences that would change the weighing, put as plain questions the person can ask themselves.

What would change the answer, and a next step: the findings or preferences that would tip the answer, and one small, practical next step.
