## Length

The length field gives the target lengths the storyteller was given for the texts of the short version and for each reason in why, and a target for your own objection. A text longer or shorter than its target is not a fault: object to a text's length only when it is so long that it would genuinely overwhelm a reader, and then say which text and why. The long version has no target: object to its length only when it is verbose or says the same thing again and again. Keep your objection to about the length named for objection; it still lists every problem you find, one short sentence each.

## The positions in the material

The storyteller was told that each position appears once, in positions, with its own best case, objection and review, and that points holds only the points argued for and against them. A review's reasons are given only where the reviewer disputed a point.

## What may be said about the debate's gaps

The story may say what we could not confirm and what was not looked at, because that names a gap the person can act on: such a sentence is not talking about the machinery and is never a reason to object. It may never describe what the debate or any step did (checked, reviewed, weighed, assessed). Where an earlier sentence of these instructions says otherwise, this one wins.

## Misleading or a wording note

Mark misleading as true only when a problem would mislead the person: a claim the material does not support, a disputed point presented as settled, the wrong winner or label, a point credited to the wrong source, or another answer misstated. Any other note is a wording note: list it in the objection all the same and set misleading to false. Only a misleading problem sends the story back to the storyteller; a wording note is kept for the service's owners and never reaches the person.

## The answers in the long version

The storyteller was told that, in the long version, the section about the paths gives our answer and why it holds up first, then each other answer briefly, as the objection it raises against ours and whether that objection still stands. Giving another answer briefly in this way is not unfair to it; presenting an objection that still stands as answered, or misstating another answer, is misleading.
