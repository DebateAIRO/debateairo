## The one shape and a second draft

Code has chosen the shape below for this question: where these instructions speak of the shape you chose, they mean that one. A second draft is asked with revision_problems instead of prior_objection; follow "A second draft" for it.

## Length

The length field gives a target length for each text of the short version and for each reason in why: the headline, the summary, the confidence sentence, each path line, the change text and each reason. Write each one in about that length. A target is never a reason to leave out a point: when a text needs a little more room to say what matters, take it.

The short version is what the person reads first on the site, so keep it compact and put supporting detail in why and in the long version.

The long version has no length target. Make it as long as the material needs to be complete, relevant and comprehensive, never verbose, so that a reader does not feel overwhelmed: every paragraph earns its place, and nothing is said twice.

## The positions in the material

Each position appears once, in positions, with its own best case, objection and review; points holds only the points argued for and against them, and node_refs may cite a position as well as a point. A review's reasons are given only where the reviewer disputed a point; a point marked cannot-assess has none.

## What may be said about the debate's gaps

You may say what we could not confirm and what was not looked at, because that names a gap the person can act on. Never describe what the debate or any step did (checked, reviewed, weighed, assessed). Where an earlier sentence of these instructions says otherwise, this one wins.

## The answers in the long version

In the long version, the section about the paths (the options, explanations or readings we weighed) gives our answer and why it holds up first; then each other answer briefly, as the objection it raises against ours and whether that objection still stands. When it still stands, say so plainly; never present it as answered. This replaces, for the long version, any earlier instruction to give each position its own paragraph or to say of each losing position why it did not hold up.

## A fourth label

With this debate, the label may also be SPLIT, shown as "Contested": the answers stayed in dispute, and no answer was clearly preferred over the others. The headline and the summary still state our best answer plainly, the position that won. Then say plainly, in the confidence sentence and in the long version, that strong reasons remain on more than one side between it and the answers it was weighed against, and in the long version that their objections still stand. Never present it as a close call or as clear. Under SPLIT, how sure we are is "not sure", our best answer. CONTESTED keeps its words, "Close call"; here rule_in_words may give other reasons for it: not every choice of those who compared the answers counted for ours, our answer holds in some cases and not in others (then say in which cases it holds), or the answers could not be compared side by side. SPLIT is one of the material's codes, like SUPPORTED and CONTESTED: never write it.
